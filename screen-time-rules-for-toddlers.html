Screen time rules for toddlers that actually stick

Most screen time battles aren't about the rule, they're about the surprise. A toddler can't feel ten minutes passing, so every ending feels sudden. Rules that stick are the ones a small child can see and count.

Decide before the TV goes on

Agree the amount while everyone is calm, not when the credits roll. “Two cartoons, then bath” said at the start is a promise; said at the end it's a punishment.

Count episodes, not minutes

Under-fives understand “two more” long before they understand a clock. Episodes have a natural ending, so there is no cutting off a story halfway through.

Keep it the same every day

Same time, same number, same next step. Children relax when they know what's coming, and after a week the rule stops being discussed.

Warn before the last one

A clear “this is the last cartoon” at the start of the final episode gives your child the whole episode to get used to the idea.

End with something good

Make the next thing appealing: bubbles in the bath, a favourite story, helping to set the table. Turning off is easier when there is something to turn to.

Use the guidance as a guide

The World Health Organization suggests no more than one hour of sedentary screen time a day for 2–4 year olds, and less is better. Choose a number that works for your family and stick to it.
