Counting cartoons vs. visual timers

Most screen-time tools count minutes. Toddlers don't. Here's why counting cartoons works better for young children, and how One More Cartoon! compares with visual timers and device screen-time limits.

Why minutes don't work for toddlers

Children under about seven can't feel how long “ten more minutes” is, so the end of screen time always arrives as a surprise. A visual timer helps a little, but it still runs out whenever it runs out, often halfway through an episode, which is the fastest route to tears.

Why counting cartoons does

“Two more cartoons” is something a three-year-old can see and count. The end becomes predictable, the last episode finishes properly, and the limit feels like a rule of the game rather than a parent's mood.

Questions parents ask

What is the difference between One More Cartoon! and a visual timer?

A visual timer counts minutes. One More Cartoon! counts cartoons: you agree a number of episodes, the app warns before the last one with a cheerful voice and then announces what comes next, so screen time ends at the end of a story instead of in the middle of one.

Why count cartoons instead of minutes?

Children under about seven can't feel how long ten minutes is, but they can count two or three cartoons. A number they can see and count makes the end predictable, and predictable endings are calmer.

Does One More Cartoon! block the TV or other apps?

No. It works alongside any TV, YouTube, Netflix or streaming app and does not control them. It is a friendly countdown, not a lock.
