How to end screen time without tantrums

If turning off the TV ends in tears most evenings, you're not alone. Young children aren't being difficult on purpose: they simply can't see the end coming. These seven steps make the end of screen time predictable, and predictable endings are much calmer.

1. Count cartoons, not minutes

Under-sevens can't feel how long ten minutes is, so the end always arrives as a surprise. Agree a number of cartoons or episodes before the screen goes on: something they can see and count.

2. Agree the plan before you press play

Children accept limits much better when they helped set them. Ask “How many cartoons tonight, two or three?” and let them choose from options you're happy with.

3. Give a warning before the last one

A clear “this is the last cartoon” gives children time to get ready for the change, instead of having the screen taken away mid-episode.

4. Say what comes next, and make it appealing

Ending screen time is easier when it leads somewhere: “after the last cartoon, it's bath time with the boats”. Naming the next activity turns an ending into a transition.

5. Stop at the end of an episode, not in the middle

Cutting off a story halfway is the fastest route to tears. Short episodes (5–12 minutes) make it easy to stop at a natural ending.

6. Let something else be the bad guy

When a timer, a song or a friendly voice announces the end, the limit feels like a rule of the game, not a parent's mood, and you get to be the one who celebrates with them.

7. Be consistent

Use the same routine every day. After a few days most children start to expect “last cartoon, then bath” and the protests shrink.
